## 2. Defects found, in priority order

### P1 — Cadastral demonstration panel is not labelled as a demonstration

**Author's intent (clarified 2026-08-01):** this panel is deliberately a
*preview of the future capability* — showing what a parcel-level report will
look like once field boundaries (मेड़) are available. That is a legitimate
thing to ship. The finding below is therefore not "remove it" but "label it".

Clicking the bottom **Cadastral** tab renders a panel headed
`सीमांक CADASTRAL — SAHIJANA | KHASRA`, with a dropdown reading
**"-- Choose from 1,000 Parcels --"** and labelled fields for
`LAND RECORD (B-1)`, `KHASRA NO`, `AREA (Ha)`, `FARMER IDENTITY`,
`OWNER NAME`, `FATHER/HUSBAND`.

The fields are empty, but the interface asserts that 1,000 parcels exist for a
village named SAHIJANA. No such data is in the repository.

Cause: this markup is hardcoded in `dashboard/index.html` (lines 1007 and
1013), left over from the synthetic version. `cadastral_loader.js` is the
correct stub and does replace the pane with a "pending official data" notice,
but `loadCadastralLayer()` is only invoked from the sidebar navigation route
(`setNav`), not from the bottom-tab route (`switchTab`). So the tab shows the
raw shell.

Nothing on screen marks this as a preview. A reviewer from NABARD or a revenue
department who opens this tab has no way to tell it apart from a live module,
and will reasonably conclude the portal holds 1,000 parcel records for a
village called Sahijana. Every other panel in the portal states "Not
available" when data is absent, which makes this one read as an exception
rather than a mockup.

**Fix — keep the panel, label it:**

1. Add a persistent banner at the top of the pane:
   *"DEMONSTRATION — structural preview of the planned cadastral module. Real
   khasra boundaries will be loaded from MP Bhulekh / Bhu-Naksha. No value
   shown here is an actual land record."* (bilingual)
2. Replace `-- Choose from 1,000 Parcels --` with `-- Sample parcel (demo) --`.
   Do not state a parcel count.
3. Replace the hardcoded village name `SAHIJANA` with `Sample village`, or
   leave it blank. A real village name attached to non-existent records is the
   most misleading element.
4. Leave `OWNER NAME` and `FATHER/HUSBAND` as empty labelled fields annotated
   `[MP Bhulekh field]`. Never populate them, even with example names —
   these are personal-data fields and a plausible name is indistinguishable
   from a real record.
5. Keep the schema (khasra no, area, crop, soil type, irrigation source)
   visible. Showing the intended structure is the legitimate purpose of this
   panel and demonstrates readiness to integrate.

Done this way the panel becomes an asset in review rather than a liability:
it shows the integration is designed and waiting, and that the project
distinguishes clearly between what it has and what it plans.

### P2 — "Rainfall Deficit: 1369 mm" is mislabelled

The metric card reads **Rainfall Deficit 1369 mm** for Jabalpur. 1369 mm is
Jabalpur's mean annual rainfall (the same number appears correctly as
`ANNUAL RAIN` in the historical indices panel below). It is not a deficit.

A rainfall deficit is a departure from a long-period normal, expressed as a
negative anomaly in mm or as a percentage below normal. Presenting total
rainfall under a "deficit" label inverts the meaning: 1369 mm is close to
Jabalpur's normal, so the correct deficit is near zero.

**Fix:** either rename the card to "Annual rainfall" or compute a real
departure: `annual_rain_mm − mean(annual_rain_mm, 2000–2024)`, shown with sign
and percentage.

### P3 — "Heatwave Severity: EXTREME" contradicts the underlying index

The card shows **EXTREME** with the note "43.3 °C Jabalpur". The same district
records 1.1 heatwave days per year and 0.1 severe heatwave days per year,
which is low by IMD criteria. The severity label appears to be derived from
absolute maximum temperature alone rather than from the computed heatwave
indices.

**Fix:** drive the severity class from `heatwave_days` and
`severe_heatwave_days` against stated thresholds, and publish those thresholds
in `docs/METHODOLOGY.md`. If absolute Tmax is intended as the trigger, label
the card "Max recorded Tmax" instead of "Heatwave Severity".

### P4 — Sidebar entries route to unrelated panels

Clicking **Soil Moisture** in the sidebar highlights that entry but opens the
NDVI time-series chart. The sidebar advertises 16 sections
(Climate Risk Atlas, NDVI Analytics, Rainfall Monitor, Soil Moisture,
Heat Waves, Extreme Precipitation, Drought, Groundwater & Irrigation,
Crop Health, Farmer Advisory, PMFBY Insurance, Forest Monitor,
Biodiversity Risk, AI Forecasting, Village Intelligence, Dashboard), but
several are shortcuts into the same bottom-tab set rather than distinct
modules.

**Fix:** audit every sidebar entry. Either point it at a panel that matches
its label, or mark it visibly as "coming soon" with the data source it awaits.
A menu that promises a soil-moisture module and delivers an NDVI chart reads
as unfinished.

### P5 — Static status badges

`Climate Risk Atlas` carries a **LIVE** badge and `Heat Waves` an **ALERT**
badge. Neither is driven by data. An "ALERT" that is always on is
indistinguishable from decoration and undermines any real alert later.

**Fix:** derive the badge from the current year's index for the selected
district, or remove it.

### P6 — NDVI chart renders nearly empty for Jabalpur

The DiCRA NDVI time series (2013–2026) shows only a narrow band at the left
edge of the plot. Either the series is not being bound to the chart for this
district, or the axis range is wrong.

**Fix:** verify `dicra_ndvi_loader.js` against `data/dicra_ndvi.json` for the
`jabalpur` key.

### P7 — Entry gate has no guest route

The gate requires a first name before the dashboard is reachable. There is no
"Skip" or "Continue as guest". For a public research portal intended for
government review, this is friction at the worst possible moment, and it sits
awkwardly against the platform's own transparency commitment.

**Fix:** add a "Skip / अतिथि के रूप में देखें" link that dismisses the gate.

### P8 — AOI results cannot be exported

The AOI panel computes statistics over up to several thousand villages and
offers no way to take them away.

**Fix:** add a "Download CSV" button exporting one row per village inside the
polygon, with the AOI area, perimeter, generation timestamp and source string
in a header comment.

## 3. What is still missing for a national-grade platform

These are not defects. They are the gap between the present portal and the
stated ambition.

### Data coverage

| Gap | Blocked on | Lead time |
|---|---|---|
| Cadastral parcels (khasra) | MP Bhulekh / Bhu-Naksha data agreement | 1–3 months — **apply first** |
| Mandi prices, MSP, arrivals | data.gov.in API key (AGMARKNET) | immediate, free |
| Groundwater levels | CGWB / India-WRIS | 2–6 weeks |
| Soil health and nutrients | Soil Health Card, NBSS&LUP | 1–2 months |
| Sentinel-2 / Landsat / SMAP indicators | GEE service account | 1–3 days |
| Village socioeconomic profile | Census India, Agriculture Census | varies |
| Forest and biodiversity | FSI, India Biodiversity Portal | varies |

### Scientific depth

- Record is 25 years (2000–2024) against the WMO 30-year normal. Extending
  IMD back to 1995 closes this and is purely a data-fetch job.
- One scenario only (SSP2-4.5). Four SSPs across 5/10/20/30-year horizons is a
  parameter change in `scripts/05_gee_cmip6_2040.js`.
- Village values are nearest-pixel samples of a 5.5 km grid.
  `config.VILLAGE_SAMPLE_METHOD = "polygon"` is scaffolded but not implemented.
- No trained model exists. Any yield, suitability or risk score must ship with
  training/test split, RMSE, MAE, R², ROC-AUC and SHAP before it is published.

### Coverage and delivery

- All-India block and village boundaries cannot ship as raw GeoJSON
  (8–15 GB). Convert to PMTiles with `tippecanoe` and serve from object
  storage; see `docs/GO_LIVE_PLAN.md`.
- No backend. AGMARKNET, CGWB and Bhulekh all require server-side keys or a
  CORS proxy.

### Institutional readiness

- No disclaimer page. Required before any public or government launch:
  indices derive from a 5.5 km grid; advisories are informational and not a
  substitute for KVK or extension guidance; cadastral displays are not legal
  land records.
- No licence attribution page for IMD, DiCRA and Census.
- No uptime monitoring or database backup.
- No independent methodology review. If NABARD, ICAR or NITI Aayog endorsement
  is the goal, arrange review before launch rather than after.

## 4. Recommended order of work

1. Fix P1 (cadastral shell). It is the only finding that misrepresents the
   platform's data holdings, and it takes under an hour.
2. Fix P2 and P3. Wrong scientific labels cost more credibility than missing
   features.
3. Register on data.gov.in and submit the MP Bhulekh application the same day.
   The application has the longest lead time of anything on this list.
4. Fix P4–P8.
5. Extend IMD to 1995; run four SSP scenarios.
6. Write the disclaimer and attribution pages.
7. Only then begin model training.

---

## 5. Assessment

The portal is scientifically defensible for the five districts it covers. The
AOI analysis, the provenance panel, the honest "Not available" states and the
CI guard against synthetic data are all genuinely above the standard of most
government dashboards.

The remaining risk is not the code. It is that a handful of leftover labels
and one leftover interface still claim more than the data supports. Those are
what a reviewer will find first, and they are cheap to fix.
